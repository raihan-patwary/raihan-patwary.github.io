for k, (_, test_rows) in enumerate(splitter.split(df, df["weight_class"])):

LarvaLens

Whole rearing batches go to one fold, so every test is on unseen batches.

def assign_batch_folds(batches: pd.DataFrame, n_folds: int = 5, seed: int = 42) -> dict[str, int]:
    """Assign whole batches to folds, spreading each temperature group across folds."""
    rng = np.random.default_rng(seed)
    order = batches.assign(tiebreak=rng.random(len(batches)))
    group_size = order.groupby("temp_group")["batch"].transform("count")
    order = order.assign(group_size=group_size).sort_values(
        ["group_size", "temp_group", "n", "tiebreak"], ascending=[False, True, False, True]
    )
    rows = np.zeros(n_folds)
    per_group: dict[str, np.ndarray] = {}
    out: dict[str, int] = {}
    for rec in order.itertuples(index=False):
        counts = per_group.setdefault(rec.temp_group, np.zeros(n_folds))
        noise = rng.random(n_folds) * 1e-6
        fold = int(np.lexsort((noise, rows, counts))[0])
        out[rec.batch] = fold
        counts[fold] += 1
        rows[fold] += rec.n
    return out

How I like to work

Data first, model second, fair test always

Every project follows the same five steps. I am honest about where I am strong and where I am still growing.

  1. Start from a farm questionHow heavy is this animal? How will this pen finish?
  2. Clean and prepare the dataCheck every number, fix what is wrong, document what is doubtful.Strong zone
  3. Explore and set a fair testPlot first, then hold out whole animals, pens or batches.
  4. Choose the right modelCompare simple and deep models and pick the most accurate.Growing zone
  5. Build the final pipelineTurn the analysis into a clean, reusable tool.Growing zone

Where I am strongest

Data cleaning and preprocessing: auditing messy farm and lab records, finding the errors in them, and getting data ready for a fair analysis.

What I am sharpening now

Selecting the most accurate model with confidence, and building complete, production-ready pipelines. This is what I most want to learn in my next step.

How I started learning machine learning

Thank you, StatQuest. BAM!

The book that opened the door for me was The StatQuest Illustrated Guide to Machine Learning by Josh Starmer. It explains each idea with drawings and small steps, and cheers you on with a "BAM!" every time something clicks. Least squares, residuals and R² stopped being formulas and became pictures I could reason with.

The doodle is mine: a mealworm, my first research animal, fitting a line. The style tips its hat to the book.
