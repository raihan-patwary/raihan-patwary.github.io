Broilers fed cricket meal

Broiler growth and feed efficiency with dietary cricket meal: an open trial dataset, checked and reanalysed.

Code and notebooks on GitHub
Data35-day trial, 6 diets, 24 pens (Mendeley Data)
Unit of analysisThe pen
Toolspandas, SciPy, scikit-learn

Why it matters

Cricket meal is tested as a protein source for broilers. An open 35-day trial (six diets from 0 to 20% cricket meal, 24 pens) made it possible to check how growth and feed efficiency responded, and how early a pen's final weight can be forecast.

What I did

  • Inspected every sheet before changing anything, and recomputed every value the workbook supplied (gain, feed conversion, organ indices, yields).
  • Compared diets with the pen, not the bird, as the experimental unit: one-way ANOVA with Holm-adjusted comparisons against the control.
  • Forecast final pen weight with simple models (straight lines, a Gompertz growth curve, nearest neighbours, random forest), each tested on pens it had not seen.

What I found

  • The workbook's gains contradict its weights in 14 of 120 pen-weeks, and 21 organ indices do not match the listed weights.
  • Final weight differed among diets (P = 0.021); only the 5% cricket meal pens were lighter than the control (7.7%). Feed intake and FCR showed no clear difference.
  • From day 28, a straight line forecast an unseen pen's final weight within 26 g (1.3%); the random forest did worse.

Keep in mind

With four pens per diet, only large differences can be detected. The notebooks show every check, so anyone can rerun them.

Data: doi:10.17632/rj5fybnzh6.1. Analysis and figures are my own.
